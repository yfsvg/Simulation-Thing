# Game plan - Homebound

The ultimate goal of the project is to build a spaceship through a tech tree that can let you progress and return to Earth.

The lore of the game is that you're a hyperintelligent AI system, in control of a group of a small group of nine robots to start off. Supposed to be an RTS game

We're gonna actually change up the system so it's not autonomously locking onto tasks. You drag and click your mouse to assign tasks to individual bots. There will be an in-game day/night cycle, during the night time you can adapt your tech tree and make other changes and stuff. 

You'll need to go to the poles to get water and fluid, which will be SUPER important.

## Core Game Loop
You are the mind of a hyperintelligent AI system stranded on Mars. The camera is your attention, and you just look around. 

Daytime is for execution, mining, and building. Nighttime is for upgrading and data salvaging. (The way that newer players are gonna get helped throughout the tech tree, basically just dropping hints on how to use stuff). 

There will be difficulty sliders for more replayability, letting you buff or make easier certain parts of the game, although there will also be general difficulty categories which players are encouraged to try on their first playthrough.

Just a flat 2d map accompanied by a little 3d window showing what part of the plan you have focused in your fov should be enough for the game for now.

## What the player controls
Clicking a bot or dragging over a region of bots highlights them, lets player to move them to different places and change their targets. 

Robots bring back raw materials, player crafts things and infrastructure with them, although steps are usually needed to get things started. Similar mechanics to Minecraft Create Mod, which is a big inspiration for this game. You can make infrastructure like rads, rail, pipelines, cables. Fabricator bots build them.

There will be a priority line for each type of bot, and you can move and allocate bots do to certain types of tasks.

## The bots themselves
There are four bots you start off with. The driller, scout, fabricator, and hauler*. (Maybe edit hauler). Upgrade is usually needed for each bot. 

Bots that don't make it home before nighttime or run out of battery die and remain on the map, although its possible to mine those and get some scrap metal back. 

An upgrade halfway through lets you name them!

## Resources that you can find, in what order you get them at
Dry resources (early game)
1. Regolith: everywhere; sintered into bricks.
2. Iron oxide -> iron -> steel: will be the main structural chain for things that you craft with and create
3. Silicon: solar panels and chips.
4. Perchlorates: toxic contaminant, later an oxidizer source.
5. Heavy elements: from far craters, caves, meteorite falls. Which also encourages exploration. Uranium is one of them I know I'll add, fuels fission which will be an end-game energy source.

Abstract resources (All game)
1. Power: the universal constraint; stored in batteries, never stockpiled infinitely.
2. Compute: generated by server racks, spent on research and command capacity.
3. Heat: electronics must stay warm at night and cool under load.

Fluids (mid-late game)
Water, hydrogen, oxygen, liquid CO2, methane, coolant, brine, lubricant, acid.

## What happens at night
Once night falls, the user first receives a small briefing on the days progress. How many units returned, how many resources were gathered, how much land explored, etc.

The research screen appears with the tech tree, spending the day's data and compute power to research certain stuff. 

Blueprint mode, laying out tomorrow's buildings and network lines as ghosts, waiting for progress.

Reorganizing prioirities for each type of bot. For ex, if you really want a new fluid tank rather than fixing a conveyer belt, go ahead, put the fluid tank on top for the robots.

## Infrastructure
Roads for commonly traveled areas, that way you can reduce the risk of tripping over and hazards that come as you travel. 

Ice conveyers will be the first way that players get water from the poles, although it would be inefficient.

Fluid tanks need to be heated.
Pipelines are for late game, they allow for massive fluid transfer and require heat padding and insulation. Pumps are needed at the start and end of each pipeline in order for it to function.

Machinery can be used to excavate a lot of ground, which can give materials.

Cables can allow canyons to be crossed by individual bots.

Depots and warehouses can store cold materials that don't need to be heated, essentially just stored out in the open. Build sites need one of these nearby for fabricators make building easier. They naturally just go to the closest depot to collect items. 

Rails can transfer large amounts of solid material.

There is a certain range in which the robots can still be actively guided. For example, in exploration mode where they are far away, they are grayscaled slightly and act entirely autonomously. Signal receivers can expand this range.

Building can happen at any time. When you make a decision to build something, a ghost blueprint will form and fabricators will work on it.

## Energy / Electricity
Solar panels will be easy to construct and scale up (Linear expansion), although poor in terms of space. Will be early game.

Wind energy will be able to get small amounts of energy passively, but are crucial for surviving dust storms that happen overnight randomly, and receive a massive boost during that period.

Geothermal energy, for mid game. Best in strategic hotspots like near volcanos, which further encourages infrastructure development.

Fission/nuclear energy, using uranium found in craters and lots of technology, creates a crap ton of energy necessary to create the final spaceship.

## Hazards and danger
The game is themed to be a semi-chill RTS game so no constant wave attacks from martian mega spiders or whatever, mostly just environmental hazards. 

Dust storms are the most deadly. Forecaster infrastructure devices can pick them up minutes to days in advance depending on how many you have built and in what areas. Bots that are caught lacking and don't return in time as ordered by you (For ex, out exploring out of your range), will shut off at best or be totally wrecked depending on their health/durability.

Regular dust wear as certain elements slowly turn red and they lose efficiency, down to half as effective before totally breaking and just not working. Fabricator bots can clean/fix them.

Individual bots also can become rusty or get covered with dust, which also makes them slower or reduces efficiency.

There can also be wild-card hazards that happen
1. Resistance from groups bots if you've treated many of them poorly or send too many on suicide missions. They will just refuse to work for a certain period of time.
2. Mutiny if you continually ignore resistance and pressure more to go on tasks, active sabotage and removing progress that you will have to either spend compute power to turn them back normal or just straight up shut down, effectively wasting a bot. (This happens only after extreme cognition upgrades)
3. Dust devils, actually relatively slow moving but you'll have to move bots away if they don't have vision/cognition upgrades. 
4. Volcanic eruptions. Every so often one of the volcanos that you have access to will erupt. You will have ample warning if you have a geothermal plant nearby anyways, so you have time to adapt to it and disassemble the plant for the energy quickly.
